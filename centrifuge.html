Outside sample memo: Centrifuge

Based only on public materials, checked 7 October 2026. Not affiliated with Centrifuge.

Written by Nikola

A shorter contrast to the Maple plan, built around one question: who is this community for now?

What the public product is now

Centrifuge's homepage describes itself as open infrastructure for onchain asset management.[1] It sorts visitors into two groups. Issuers and builders get white-label tokenization, deRWA tokens for distribution across DeFi, and full-service fund management through Anemoy, its asset management arm. Investors access tokenized assets through the app.[1]

The docs welcome page says the same thing in builder language: pre-built modules for tokenization, NAV and investor management, an SDK and API, and ERC-4626 and ERC-7540 assets that plug into DeFi protocols.[2] The featured news is about asset managers: Janus Henderson and SMT using Centrifuge vaults, and New York Life Investment Management tokenizing a high-yield bond strategy.[1]

Read plainly, the product today is software for institutions launching tokenized funds, plus a distribution layer into DeFi. The docs call the current generation Centrifuge V3, a multi-chain product deployable on any EVM chain.[3]

The shift, and where the site still shows it

The Legacy page tells the story openly. The 2017 system represented off-chain receivables, such as invoices or loan agreements, as tokens. Tinlake, in 2020, was a securitization engine with junior TIN and senior DROP tranches. V2 moved to a Substrate chain connected to Polkadot. V3 is the EVM product above.[3]

The shift still shows in two places a newcomer reaches quickly. The Mission and History page opens with the cost of securitization intermediaries for small and medium enterprises. Its history ends in late 2023, under a heading that still reads "looking ahead", with pools launching on Centrifuge Chain.[4] Meanwhile the token page says CFG is now an Ethereum ERC-20, Centrifuge Chain is deprecated, and the legacy migration is permanently closed.[5] So one page in the docs sidebar describes a chain that another page, two links further down, calls deprecated.

Who the community is for now

Builders and issuers. These are the people the homepage and docs actually speak to. Their rooms are the docs, GitHub and the contact form.[1][2]

Investors in tokenized products. They need the app and the information for each product, not general chat.[1]

CFG holders. Under CP171, approved on 3 November 2025, active DAO governance is paused and the Centrifuge Network Foundation supervises execution. The docs describe the community's role as local activations, events and grassroots initiatives, and keep a process for reinstating the DAO.[6] That is a narrower role than "governance participant", and community messaging should match it.

Who the old framing no longer fits. Small businesses looking for invoice financing. Anyone expecting Tinlake pools or TIN and DROP tokens. Parachain-era users. Holders of legacy CFG or WCFG hoping to migrate.[3][5] These people will keep arriving, because older articles still circulate. They deserve a short, kind, final answer, not a debate.

One routing detail: the homepage footer and the governance docs give two different Discord invite links, and only the docs list a Telegram group.[1][6] From outside I can't tell whether both invites lead to the same server. Confirming one canonical invite would be my first task.

What I would stop saying

  • "Invoice financing for SMEs." True as history, wrong as a pitch.[3]
  • "Tinlake", "DROP" or "parachain", anywhere except a pointer to the Legacy page.[3]
  • "Put it to a vote." Active DAO governance is paused. The honest line is how reinstatement works.[6]
  • "You may still be able to migrate." The docs say the migration is permanently closed.[5]
  • Two headline numbers used as if they were one. The homepage shows "1.8B+" labelled Total Value Locked.[1] The docs welcome page says more than $3.5B in real-world assets have been tokenized through the platform.[2] Both are Centrifuge's own figures, checked 7 October 2026, but one is a current balance and the other a running total. Swap them in a reply and someone will call one of them false.
  • Anything quoted from the history page as "looking ahead". That section describes late 2023.[4]
